Whether a granny flat can be built on your property is decided before any floor plan is chosen. In most parts of Brisbane and South East Queensland, the answer comes down to a handful of practical questions.

## 1. Is there a practical place to build?

A large backyard doesn't automatically mean there's a suitable building area. What matters is the space that remains after the practical constraints are accounted for:

- Setbacks required from boundaries
- The existing home, sheds and other structures
- Easements and underground services
- Flood, vegetation and other property constraints
- Space for parking and private open space where required

The important question isn't how large the backyard looks, but how much **usable building space** remains.

## 2. Can construction reach the site?

A workable building area still needs a practical construction route. Machinery, materials, concrete and trades all need to reach the backyard:

- Access width and height through gates and fences
- Trees, branches and overhead services
- Road access and delivery conditions
- Space for unloading, storage and machinery

Finding space for the building is only half the assessment — you also need a workable way to build it.

## 3. What site conditions could change the design or budget?

Some conditions don't stop a granny flat from being built, but they change the foundations, drainage, service connections or construction method:

- Slope and level changes
- Soil conditions and retaining work
- Stormwater and site drainage
- Distance to sewer, water and power connections

Identifying these early matters, because they're the difference between a realistic proposal and unexpected costs later.

## 4. What do council rules say for your area?

Every council sets its own limits on secondary dwellings, and they differ from one area to the next — even between neighbouring suburbs. Rules on size, height, setbacks and approvals are decided by your local authority, not by a floor plan catalogue.

You don't need to work these out on your own. Part of what a builder does is check what applies to your specific block and tell you plainly where things stand.
