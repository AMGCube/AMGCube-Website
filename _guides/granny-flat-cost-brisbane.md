"How much does a granny flat cost?" is usually the first question — and it's one worth answering properly. A realistic budget needs to cover the whole project, not just the home itself.

Typical completed projects from AMGCUBE start from **$250,000**, including design, engineering, approvals, construction and handover. That starting point already reflects a full picture — which is why the final number for your block can still sit above or below it.

## Why similar granny flats cost differently

Two homes of the same size and layout can come in at quite different prices, because the floor plan is only part of the figure. The rest is decided by your property:

- **The site.** Access, slope, soil, retaining work and how far services have to run all affect foundations, drainage and construction effort.
- **Approvals.** Design, engineering, certification and any council or authority requirements vary from one area to the next.
- **External works.** Driveways, landscaping and other works outside the building footprint are often the least predictable part of a budget.
- **Finishes and inclusions.** The level of finish inside the home moves the number up or down more than most people expect.

## The home is only part of the budget

A 2 bedroom granny flat typically sits in the **60–100 m²** range. But the completed project also includes:

- Design, engineering and consultant fees
- Required approvals and certification
- Site preparation, foundations and drainage
- Connections for sewer, water and power
- Construction, fixtures, fittings and handover

This is why we look at the property before confirming a budget. Once the site and scope are understood, we prepare a **fixed-price proposal** — not a moving estimate that creeps up as the project goes along.

## What to watch for in a granny flat quote

When comparing quotes, look beyond the headline figure:

1. Does it include approvals and certification, or are they quoted separately?
2. Is site work and service connection included?
3. Is the proposal fixed-price once the scope is agreed?
4. What happens if a site condition shows up after construction starts?
