Site Feasibility

Can the property support the build?

We work through three practical questions: where the building could fit, how construction could reach it, and which site conditions may materially change the cost.

01

Is there enough usable space?

The question is not how large the backyard looks, but whether a suitably sized and shaped building area remains after the property constraints are considered.

A large backyard does not always mean there is enough usable space to build.
  • Backyard dimensions and shape
  • Existing home, structures and facilities
  • Setbacks from boundaries and the main house
  • Site coverage, parking and related requirements
  • Flood, vegetation and other applicable overlays
  • Easements and underground services
02

Can the backyard be practically accessed?

A workable building area still needs a realistic construction route for machinery, materials, concrete and trades.

Finding space for the building is one question. Getting machinery, materials and trades to that space is another.
  • Clear access width and height
  • Gates, eaves, fences and other obstructions
  • Trees, branches, overhead wires and power poles
  • Road width and large-vehicle stopping conditions
  • Front-yard unloading, storage and turning space
  • Concrete delivery, machinery and large-material handling
  • Impact on use of the existing home during construction
03

What site conditions could significantly increase the cost?

Some conditions do not prevent a build, but may change the foundations, drainage, material handling or overall construction method.

These conditions can materially increase the total cost and may affect whether the project remains economically practical.
  • Slope, level changes and soil conditions
  • Complex foundations or retaining work
  • Stormwater and site drainage requirements
  • Distance to sewer, water and power connections
  • Service protection, relocation or special construction
  • Demolition, clearing, tree work and manual material handling
An initial check identifies the next questions.

Final feasibility and approval pathways may require a surveyor, planner, engineer, building certifier, Council or other qualified professionals.
